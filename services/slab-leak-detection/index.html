Slab leak detection is the process of locating a broken water line beneath your concrete foundation without tearing up floors to look for it. It is a diagnostic service, separate from the repair, and it determines everything that follows: where to open concrete, whether a reroute makes more sense, and what the repair will actually involve.

Guessing is expensive. Every unnecessary hole in a slab costs money to open and money to patch, and it still might miss the leak. Our detection process narrows the location to within inches using sound and heat, so the repair starts exactly where it should.

Signs You Need Detection

Most homeowners call us after noticing one of the classic signs. A warm spot on the floor points to a hot water line leak. The sound of running water when every fixture is off means pressurized water is escaping somewhere. A water bill that climbs with no change in usage is the quietest and most common early warning.

Later signs are harder to ignore: damp carpet edges, baseboards pulling away from the wall, cracks appearing in tile or concrete, and a musty smell that will not go away. By the time you see water, the leak has usually been running for weeks. If any of these sound familiar, detection is the right next step.

  • Warm or hot spots on tile or concrete floors
  • Sound of running water with all fixtures off
  • Water bills rising with no change in usage
  • Damp carpet, buckling wood, or cracked tile
  • Musty odors near floors or baseboards

How Acoustic and Thermal Detection Work

Acoustic detection listens for the leak. Pressurized water escaping through a crack creates a distinct high-frequency sound that travels through the pipe and slab. Our ground microphones and listening discs amplify that sound while filtering out background noise, letting us trace the line and mark where the sound is loudest. That mark is usually within inches of the break.

Thermal imaging sees the leak. An infrared camera shows temperature differences across your floor, and a hot water leak paints a clear warm signature spreading through the concrete. Cold water leaks are harder to see thermally, which is why we use both methods together. We also isolate zones by shutting off valves, so we can confirm which branch of your plumbing is actually losing pressure.

  • Acoustic listening traces the sound of escaping water
  • Thermal imaging reveals hot water leak signatures
  • Zone isolation confirms which line is losing pressure
  • Meter testing verifies the leak before we start
  • Both methods used together for cold water lines

What Happens After We Find It

We show you the marked location and explain what we found in plain terms: which line, where it broke, and what the repair options are. You get a fixed written price for each option, spot repair, reroute, or repipe, before any work is scheduled. There is no obligation to have us do the repair.

If you move forward, we schedule the repair and handle everything from the concrete opening to the final patch. If the leak turns out to be inside a wall or at a fixture instead of under the slab, we will tell you that too, and the fix is usually simpler and cheaper than a slab repair.

  • Marked leak location shown to you on site
  • Fixed written pricing for each repair option
  • No obligation to proceed with the repair
  • Concrete opening, repair, and patching handled together
  • Written report you can share with your insurer

Frequently Asked Questions

About slab leak detection in the Lutz area.

How accurate is slab leak detection?

With acoustic and thermal equipment used together, we locate the break within inches in most cases. That accuracy is the whole point: it keeps the concrete opening small.

How long does a detection visit take?

Most visits take under two hours, including zone isolation, acoustic tracing, and thermal scanning.

Do I need to prepare my home before you arrive?

Just clear small items off the floor in the suspected area. Leave the water on so the leak is active and audible when we test.

What if you cannot find a leak?

Then you pay for the visit and get our findings in writing, which is still useful for your plumber or insurer. If the meter shows no loss, there may be no supply leak at all.

Can detection find leaks in drain lines too?

Supply lines are our specialty because they leak under pressure and cause the most damage. Drain leaks behave differently and usually need a camera inspection.

Is detection covered by insurance?

Some policies cover the cost of locating a leak when it leads to a covered repair. We provide documentation you can submit with your claim.
