A slab leak is a broken water line running beneath the concrete foundation of your home. In Lutz, most houses sit on slab-on-grade foundations, so supply lines were laid in or under the slab when the home was built. When one of those lines corrodes, cracks, or shifts, water escapes directly into the soil and concrete below your floors.

The damage is silent at first. Water erodes the soil that supports your slab, feeds mold inside wall cavities, and shows up on your water bill long before it shows up on your floor. Repair means stopping the leak at its source and restoring the line so it holds pressure again. Everything we do starts with finding the exact location, because a repair is only as good as its diagnosis.

How Slab Leak Repair Works

Every repair starts with detection. We map your plumbing layout, shut off fixtures one zone at a time, and use acoustic listening equipment to hear pressurized water escaping where it should not. Thermal imaging confirms hot water leaks by showing the heat signature spreading through the slab. By the time we mark a spot on your floor, we know where the break is within inches.

Access comes next. For a spot repair, we open the smallest section of concrete that gives us working room around the damaged pipe, usually about two feet square. We cut out the failed section, install the replacement with proper fittings, pressure test the line, then patch the concrete and restore the flooring area. For larger failures, we skip the concrete entirely and reroute the line overhead.

  • Zone-by-zone isolation to confirm which line is leaking
  • Acoustic and thermal pinpointing before any concrete is opened
  • Small, square access cuts instead of trenches across the room
  • Pressure testing before we patch anything closed
  • Concrete patching and flooring restoration included

Repair Options: Spot Repair vs Reroute vs Repipe

A spot repair fixes the single failed section of pipe. It makes sense when the rest of the line tests healthy, the home is relatively young, and this is the first leak. We open one small area, replace the bad section, and you are done. It is the least invasive and usually the fastest option.

A reroute abandons the leaking under-slab line and runs a new line through walls or the attic to the same fixtures. Nothing gets jackhammered. A repipe replaces more of the system, sometimes the whole home, and is the right call when leaks keep returning or the original piping is past its prime. We will show you what your pipes look like and recommend the option that fits, then let you decide.

  • Spot repair: best for a first leak in an otherwise healthy line
  • Reroute: best when you want no concrete demolition at all
  • Partial repipe: best for two or more leaks in the same system
  • Full repipe: best for aging galvanized or polybutylene throughout
  • Every option priced upfront before work begins

What It Costs and How Long It Takes

Cost depends on access, method, and how much pipe is involved. A single spot repair with a small concrete opening costs less than a reroute, which costs less than opening a large section of slab or repiping a home. Depth of the line, flooring type, and whether drywall needs patching all factor in. We give you a fixed written price before we start, so the number we quote is the number you pay.

Timelines are straightforward. Detection usually takes under two hours. A spot repair is typically a one-day job including the concrete patch. A reroute usually takes one to two days depending on how many fixtures are involved. Larger repipes are scheduled across several days, and we keep your water on every night except during the final tie-in.

Frequently Asked Questions

About slab leak repair in the Lutz area.

How do I know the leak is under the slab and not somewhere else?

We isolate your plumbing zone by zone. If the meter spins with everything off, the leak is in the supply system, and acoustic equipment tells us whether it is under the slab or inside a wall.

Will you have to tear up all my flooring?

No. We open only the area we need, usually about two feet square for a spot repair. If you prefer zero concrete work, a reroute avoids the slab entirely.

Can a slab leak be fixed without breaking concrete?

Yes, by rerouting the line overhead through walls or the attic. The old under-slab line is capped and abandoned in place.

How long does a slab leak repair last?

A properly installed repair with correct fittings lasts as long as the surrounding pipe. That is why we pressure test every repair before closing it up.

Is the water safe while the leak is active?

The leak is on the pressurized supply side, so water quality is generally unaffected. The risk is structural: erosion under the slab and mold growth.

Do you repair the concrete and flooring afterward?

Yes. We patch the concrete opening and restore the immediate area. Matching existing tile or specialty flooring exactly is not always possible, and we will tell you that upfront.

What should I do if I suspect a leak right now?

Turn off the main water valve if water is actively pooling, then call (813) 555-0147. If nothing is flooding, leave the water on so we can detect the leak when we arrive.
