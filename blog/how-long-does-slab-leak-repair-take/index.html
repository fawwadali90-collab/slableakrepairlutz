How Long Does Slab Leak Repair Take? Timelines Explained

When water is leaking under your foundation, the first question after what is wrong is how long you will be living with it. The answer depends on the repair method, and the honest version is refreshingly short: most slab leak work is measured in days, not weeks.

Here is the realistic timeline for each stage, from the detection visit to the final patch, based on how Wesley Chapel and Lutz jobs actually unfold.

Detection: Under Two Hours

The diagnostic visit is the fastest part. Meter testing, zone isolation, acoustic tracing, and thermal scanning usually wrap up in under two hours for a typical single-story home. You get a marked leak location, an explanation of what failed, and fixed written pricing for each repair option before we leave.

Larger or two-story homes can take a bit longer because there is more plumbing to isolate. Either way, detection is a single visit, and you will know exactly where you stand the same day.

Spot Repair: Usually One Day

A spot repair through the slab is typically a one-day job. The crew opens the concrete in the morning, cuts out the failed section and installs the replacement by midday, pressure tests the line, and patches the concrete in the afternoon. You have water back the same evening.

Flooring restoration beyond the concrete patch may add a day if tile needs to be reset, but the plumbing itself is done in one visit. This is the fastest path from diagnosis to fixed.

Reroute: One to Two Days

Rerouting a single branch usually takes one full day: running the new line through walls or attic, making connections, testing, and patching drywall openings. Multi-fixture reroutes stretch to two days.

Because there is no concrete work, reroutes are cleaner day-to-day than slab openings. Crews protect floors along their path, and the loudest part of the job is usually the drywall patching, not demolition.

  • Single-branch reroute: typically one day
  • Multi-fixture reroute: one to two days
  • No concrete demolition at any stage
  • Water restored each evening

Whole-Home Repipe: Two to Four Days

A full repipe is the biggest job on this list and still usually finishes within a work week. Two to four days covers most single-story Tampa Bay homes, with larger two-story homes at the upper end. Crews work section by section so disruption stays contained.

You will have water every evening except possibly during the final tie-in, which is scheduled to minimize the outage. Drywall patching follows the plumbing, and we coordinate it so you are not living in a construction zone any longer than necessary.

What Can Delay Your Repair

Most repairs run on schedule, but a few things can stretch the timeline. The most common is worse pipe condition than expected: we open the slab and find the neighboring pipe crumbling, which turns a spot repair into a reroute conversation. This is not a sales tactic, it is what forty-year-old copper looks like, and we show you the pipe so you can see it yourself.

Access surprises are next. Custom tile that cannot be matched, a leak under a kitchen island that needs the cabinets moved, or a post-tension slab requiring an engineer's review all add time, and permit inspections can add a day depending on the county schedule. Material lead times occasionally affect specialty fittings as well, but we flag that possibility in the quote when it applies. The best defense is a thorough detection visit and honest conversation upfront: when we quote your job, we flag the likely complications before work starts, not after, so the timeline you approve is the timeline we work to. A contractor who promises the fastest possible timeline without caveats is selling optimism, not a schedule. Ask for the realistic timeline, plan around it, and you will usually be pleasantly surprised.
