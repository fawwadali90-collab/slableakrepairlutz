Emergency Slab Leak: What to Do in the First 30 Minutes

Water is pooling on your floor and you do not know where it is coming from. The next thirty minutes determine whether this is a bad day or a five-figure disaster. This is the exact sequence to follow, in order.

Print it mentally now, because nobody thinks clearly with water rising. Every step here buys you time and money.

Minutes 0 to 5: Stop the Water

Find your main water shutoff and turn it clockwise until it stops. In most Tampa homes it is near the street by the meter, in the garage, or where the line enters the house. If you have never touched it, now is the time to learn: go look today, before you need it in a panic.

If you cannot find or turn the valve, call us immediately and we will talk you through it on the phone while dispatching. Stopping the flow is the single highest-value action you can take.

Minutes 5 to 15: Protect People and Property

Keep everyone away from standing water near electrical outlets, appliances, and your breaker panel. Water and electricity are the real danger in a flooding home, more than the plumbing itself. If water is near the panel, do not touch it; call an electrician after the water stops.

Move rugs, furniture legs, electronics, and anything irreplaceable off wet floors and onto dry ground. Open interior doors so air moves. Do not run fans directly on electrical items sitting in water.

  • Stay clear of water near outlets and the breaker panel
  • Move furniture, rugs, and electronics to dry areas
  • Do not use appliances sitting in standing water
  • Open interior doors for airflow

Minutes 15 to 30: Document and Call

Photograph everything before anything is moved or cleaned: the water, the damaged areas, the meter, the shutoff valve position. Your insurance adjuster was not there, and photos are your evidence. Take more than you think you need, including wide shots of each room.

Then call for help. Call us for the plumbing emergency and your insurance company's claims line to open a claim number. Tell both the same facts: when you found it, what you did, and what is damaged. Honest, early documentation makes claims smoother.

What Not to Do

Do not break concrete to find the leak yourself. You will not find it by guessing, and every hole you open is damage the repair crew has to work around. Do not ignore it until morning either: an active leak erodes soil and grows mold by the hour.

Do not hire the first person who knocks on your door after a storm, either. Emergency work still deserves a written price before it starts. Anyone who will not give you one is telling you something.

What to Do in the Next 24 Hours

Once the water is off and help is called, the next 24 hours are about drying and documentation. Keep the air moving with fans in dry areas, and if you have a dehumidifier, run it near the wet zone. Do not rip up flooring yourself: disturbed materials complicate both the repair and the insurance claim.

Expect two visits, not one. The emergency visit stops the damage by isolating or capping the line. The detection visit, usually scheduled within a day or two, pinpoints the break and produces the written plan and fixed price for the permanent repair. Knowing this sequence keeps the chaos organized: stop, dry, document, detect, repair. Write that sequence on a sticky note inside your breaker panel door. It takes thirty seconds now and saves thirty panicked minutes later.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I call a plumber or my insurance first?

Stop the water first, then call the plumber, then your insurer. Mitigation comes before paperwork.

Will turning off the water damage anything?

No. Shutting off the main is safe for your plumbing and your water heater. It only stops the flooding.

How fast can emergency crews arrive?

We dispatch same-day across our service area. Exact timing depends on current call volume, which we will tell you honestly on the phone.

What if the flooding stops on its own?

It did not fix itself; the pressure just equalized somewhere. The break is still there. Schedule detection promptly.
